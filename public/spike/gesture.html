        Five dials, not thirty. Every beat of every pattern has an x, a y and a
        rebound, and the six has five path points on top — a bench exposing all of those would be a
        coordinate editor and nobody can hold a hundred numbers at once. These five describe how a
        gesture behaves; the drawn patterns stay as they are.
      

      

        Width is how far the hand travels sideways. Arcs is how
        high it rises between beats. Downbeat is the height the downbeat falls
        from, kept separate because scaling it with the arcs loses the downbeat at the flowing end
        and gives a baton no arm could hold at the other. Beat spread is how far
        apart the ictus points sit vertically — only the four pattern has its beats on one floor,
        so this does nothing there and a good deal everywhere else. Lag is how much
        the hand hangs between beats and snaps through them.
      

      

        What to watch for. A beat has to be approached downwards and left upwards,
        or there is no ictus and nothing to read; and it wants to be moving quicker at the beat than
        between beats, or the only cue left is the change of direction. The four pattern's second
        beat is the one that fails first — its long stroke straight across has never had much speed
        contrast to give — so if any cell looks wrong, look there first.
